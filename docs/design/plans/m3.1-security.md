# Arablyzer — M3.1: the pre-launch security review

> What the pre-launch security review found (2026-09-30), by the part of the stack it concerns: what was
> found, what changed, what was measured, and what is left. Numbers are from 2026-09-30, measured in the
> scanner image (Node 24.21, and the container limits Compose gives the scanner: 1200 MB, a V8 heap of 696 MB)
> unless a row says "host": Node 22.22 on Apple silicon. Written as the work goes.

---

## Scanner

The findings of the review in what runs a scan: **H1** (GitHub issue #28).

### H1: one page ended the scanner, and every scan queued behind it

**Found.** A page of 1.4 million `<p>` is 4 KB gzipped and 4.2 MB read, under every limit a scan had (25 MB
fetched, 15 MB parsed). Its tree took more heap than Node has in the scanner's container, and Node ended the
whole process: reproduced in the scanner image, exit status 134, "Ineffective mark-compacts near heap limit".
The worker's next requests were refused, and "the scanner is not there" was final: a scan is tried once, so
every scan queued behind the one that died failed within moments, until Compose had started the scanner again.

**Changed**, in three layers, so that no one guess has to be right about every page:

1. **Limits on the tree, in the tree adapter** (`packages/collectors/src/page.ts`). At most **200,000 nodes**
   (elements and comments: a comment is 7 bytes of page and 200 of heap) and **10,000 levels** of nesting; the
   parse ends at the first element past either, and the page is `page-too-complex`, as it is when parsing runs
   out of time. Neither reads the clock, so the same page is too complex on every machine. The nesting limit
   is what makes 100,000 nested `<div>` (0.5 MB) end at once: parse5 works through the open elements with
   many start tags, so nesting costs time quadratic in its depth, and the deadline that ended it did so after
   the full 30 s (measured on the host: 5,000 levels 0.10 s, 20,000 1.3 s, 50,000 8.3 s, 100,000 31 s).
   Chromium's own parser stops at 512 levels (`kMaximumHTMLParserDOMTreeDepth`); 10,000 leaves room for pages
   that are broken, and costs 0.3 s at most. The notice's copy no longer says "within the time limit"
   (Arabic and English; the Arabic needs the owner's reading).
2. **The HTML is read in a thread with a heap and a clock of its own** (`collectPageIsolated`, the scanner's
   `isolateParse`). The limits above cannot say which mix of nodes and bytes fits a heap: 199,994 links of
   72 bytes are 14.4 MB, pass every limit, and need 600 MB, nearly all the 696 MB that Node has in the
   container (host, the smallest heap that survives, in steps of 50 MB; before the limits, 150 MB for 200,000
   `<p>`, 250 with Arabic text, 450 for 200,000 short links, 400 for 2 million comments). And nothing in the tree adapter can stop a parse that never reaches it: one start tag with many attributes
   is quadratic in parse5's tokenizer (20,000 attributes 0.6 s, 40,000 2.9 s, 80,000 15.9 s; 15 MB of them
   would take hours), and it held the scanner's event loop, its health answer and its own hard limit the whole
   time. The thread has `resourceLimits.maxOldGenerationSizeMb` of 384; a timer ends it wherever it is
   (`terminate()`); running out of either is `page-too-complex`; any other failure is `page-unreadable`. It runs
   the same `readHtml` as the collector, so the facts are the same (tests compare them over a rich page, two
   encodings, XHTML, a truncated page, and the reports of every twentieth rule fixture). The CLI still reads in
   its own process (its bundle stays one file, and the limits above apply to it too); the scanner turns the
   thread on in `scanOptionsFrom`. The thread needs no flag of its process: it registers `tsx` itself
   (`--import tsx` did not reach a thread on Node 22.22), and `collectors` now declares `tsx`.
3. **The worker waits for a scanner that is not there** (`apps/worker/src/run.ts`, `ScannerUnavailable` in
   `packages/scanner-client`). A refused connection, a name not found, an address unreachable, a connection
   that timed out, or a 503 before the scanner sent a line: the scan has not begun, so the worker asks again
   after 0.5 s, then 1, 2, 4 and 5 s at a time, for a minute in all. Measured against Docker's restart policy,
   a container being started refuses the connection, and one waiting for a long restart has no name in Docker's
   DNS: both are "not there". Not a reset, a closed socket, a timeout after the connection, or any other status:
   those may come after the scan began, and a scan is still never run twice behind the visitor's back
   (BullMQ's `attempts` stay one). A scanner that is back within the minute
   fails nothing, and one that is not fails that scan alone.

The worker, against a real scanner container whose Node process was killed (`SIGKILL`) in the middle of a
scan, four scans in a row. Before: the scan in flight failed with no report (it had begun: "terminated"), and
the three behind it failed 7, 2 and 2 ms later, "The scanner is not there (ECONNREFUSED)", with no report.
After: the scan in flight failed the same way, and the three behind it ran, each with its report: the first
after 1.6 s, for the scanner Compose started again (two refused asks), the other two at once.

**Left.**

- The limits are measured on pages made for the purpose. No real page was measured: pages with more than a few
  thousand elements are rare, and the notice will say when one meets a limit. Count `page-too-complex` once the
  site is public, and move a limit only with a page in hand.
- A scan that was in flight when its scanner died fails: it had begun. A scanner that stays away for more than
  a minute fails each scan that waits for it, one minute at a time.
- The CLI reads in its own process, so a start tag with hundreds of thousands of attributes still holds a CLI
  scan for minutes: the machine of whoever runs it, and no one else's.
- The thread's facts are copied back into this process (structured clone): for a moment a big page's facts
  exist twice. The heap is 384 MB so that this process, the thread and the copy fit the container's 1200 MB
  with room; the browsers start after the thread has ended.
