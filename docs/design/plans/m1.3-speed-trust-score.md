## 1. Rules (47 in all after this milestone)

| Rule | Category · severity | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| `https-missing` | trust · critical | The page ends on `http:` |
| `hsts-missing` | trust · moderate | An HTTPS page sends no `Strict-Transport-Security`, or one with `max-age=0` |
| `mixed-content` | trust · serious | An HTTPS page loads scripts, styles, frames, images or media over `http:`, or has a form that sends to `http:` |
| `tls-expiring` | trust · serious | The certificate expires within 14 days, or within a third of its lifetime for certificates shorter than 42 days |
| `text-compression-missing` | speed · moderate | The page, or a script, stylesheet or data response it loads, comes without `Content-Encoding` although gzip saves at least 1,400 bytes and at least 10% or 20,000 bytes |
| `image-format-legacy` | speed · minor | A JPEG, PNG or GIF image is at least 8,192 bytes larger than its estimated size as AVIF |
| `cwv-lcp-poor` | speed · serious | CrUX: the 75th percentile of Largest Contentful Paint on phones is over 4 s |
| `cwv-inp-poor` | speed · serious | CrUX: the 75th percentile of Interaction to Next Paint on phones is over 500 ms |
| `cwv-cls-poor` | speed · serious | CrUX: the 75th percentile of Cumulative Layout Shift on phones is over 0.25 |

- **Thresholds.** The Core Web Vitals ones are Google's published "poor" limits. The compression and image ones,
  and the AVIF estimate, are those of Lighthouse 12's audits (§0); each copy says where its numbers come from, and
  the findings give the sizes measured.
- **`tls-expiring`.** Let's Encrypt's certificates last 90 days, with renewal advised every 60, and its short-lived
  ones 6 days. A fixed 14 days would flag every 6-day certificate, so the rule takes the smaller of 14 days and a
  third of the certificate's lifetime.
- **CrUX without a key** (decision 4): the three rules are not applicable, and a notice says why. With a key, the
  page's URL is asked first and its origin second; the finding says which, and the report gives the collection
  period. A page CrUX has no data for makes them not applicable; many Arabic sites are such.

## 2. The score (`packages/scoring`, Phase 1 design §4, decision 5)

- Weights by severity: critical 10, serious 5, moderate 3, minor 1, information 0; "needs review" is never deducted.
- A category's score is `100 × (1 − failed weight ÷ applicable weight)`, rounded to a whole number; applicable
  rules are those that passed or failed. Rules that did not apply or could not run are left out, and the score says
  it is partial when any could not run. A category whose applicable weight is 0 has no score.
- The overall score is the same formula over every rule.
- Scores compare only within a major version of the rule set: `RULES_VERSION` in `packages/rules`, in the report.
- The report schema 0.2.0 stays open until Phase 1 ends (nothing is published): it gains `score` (overall,
  categories, partial, rules version), `lab` (Lighthouse's version and metrics) and the CrUX period.

## 3. Facts

- `PageFacts` gains the TLS certificate's validity. `RenderedFacts` gains, bounded: the text responses that came
  without `Content-Encoding`, with their size and gzipped size (bodies read as in M1.2c, only at a known size);
  the images drawn with their format, size and natural dimensions; and requests over `http:` from an HTTPS page.
- CrUX and Lighthouse facts come in M1.3b; the engine passes them to rules as `Evidence.crux` and to the report.

## 4. The methodology (`docs/methodology.md`, Arabic first)

The formula and weights, one worked example for each weight, and the limits of each source: browser versions and
fonts, CrUX coverage, Lighthouse's variance, WebKit on Linux not being Safari (Phase 1 design §8).

## 5. Golden reports (M1.3c)

Twenty local fixture pages that between them fail every rule, scanned with `--render` in the Docker image. The
reports are committed; a test scans the pages again and compares, leaving out times and versions. A change needs
explicit approval in its PR (BUILD-PLAN §16.3).

## 6. Tests

- Each rule: wrong and right fixtures, a detector test, Arabic and English copy.
- The scoring formula on hand-made results, with a worked example per weight matching the methodology.
- `safeFetch` POST: bounded body, redirects refused, the key never in a URL or an error.
- Lighthouse: the request limit, the proxy, the 60 s cap, a page that never loads; CrUX: a local stand-in for the
  API with each answer (data, URL missing, origin missing, errors).

## 7. For the owner

- The CrUX API key: created in Google Cloud and passed as `ARABLYZER_CRUX_API_KEY`. The URL scanned is sent to
  Google, so the privacy page will say so (Phase 2).
- The Arabic copy of the nine rules, on a review page.

**Changes from the plan, and why**

- **Fixture sites over HTTPS, under host names.** A test certificate authority made with node:crypto signs each
  fixture site's certificate. Tests trust it in their own process, or through `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS` for the CLI's
  child process, so no code of Arablyzer's needs a way to trust anything else. A site's `site.json` gives its host
  name (`shop.example`), which the tests' resolver maps to 127.0.0.1, and its certificate's window.
- **The certificate comes from the page fetch's own TLS socket**, with the time it was checked, so `tls-expiring`
  judges it at that time. The rule fails at 14 days, our choice: Certbot renews at 30, so 14 days left means
  renewal has failed for two weeks. A shorter certificate fails at a third of its lifetime.
- **`https-missing` leaves out local development hosts** (loopback and private addresses, `.localhost`, `.test`,
  `.local`, `.internal`, `.home.arpa`), which no public site has; **`hsts-missing` leaves out IP addresses**,
  for which browsers keep no HSTS.
- **`mixed-content` treats each load as browsers do** (Mixed Content §4.1): other images, audio and video are
  upgraded; everything else is blocked, images chosen by `srcset` or `<picture>` or on an IP address included. A
  page that asks for `upgrade-insecure-requests` passes, forms included (Upgrade Insecure Requests §4.1).
- **The speed rules read a body only within a bound known beforehand**: Resource Timing's decoded size, or the
  bytes that came over the network. Content-Length is no bound: a chunked or HTTP/2 body can be longer than it
  says. Chromium and WebKit hand text re-encoded as UTF-8, so text in a legacy encoding that came back longer
  than it was sent is not gzipped, and a stylesheet so re-encoded is read as UTF-8.
- **`image-format-legacy` uses Lighthouse 12's fallback for every image.** Lighthouse 12 re-encoded each image in
  Chrome and estimated AVIF from those sizes; its estimate from pixels, used here, was for images it could not
  re-encode. The pixels are the file's own: an image chosen by `srcset` or `<picture>` is measured through a
  detached image of the same URL, which the browser gives at once from its list of available images, without a
  new request (Chromium, Firefox and WebKit). An engine without the file at hand leaves the image out: Firefox,
  in the render. JPEG, PNG and BMP, as Lighthouse 12 read them; not GIF.
- **The score says which rules it covers** (`score.rules`: how many ran, of the rule set's), since a scan without
  rendering or with named rules runs fewer; every category the scan ran rules of has a score, null when none of
  them applied.
- **A rule that reads the page's files needs `files`.** When the step that reads them fails as a whole, the
  engine that failed is not read, and with none left the rule reports `files-unread`, which makes the score
  partial, instead of passing.
- **The SEO self-audit leaves out the three rules of the server** (`https-missing`, `hsts-missing`,
  `tls-expiring`): it serves the template pages locally over HTTP.

An independent review read the diff and ran the tests. Each finding was reproduced, or probed in the three
engines, before its fix.

| # | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | An image chosen by `srcset` or `<picture>` gave its size divided by its density, which shrank the AVIF estimate: a false failure | The file's own pixels, through a detached image (above); tests for `2x`, `w` with `sizes`, and `<picture>` |
| 2 | Content-Length bounded the body read: 100,004 bytes came under `Content-Length: 10` with chunked framing, in all three engines | The bound is Resource Timing's size or the bytes received; a unit test checks that such a body is never read |
| 3 | `mixed-content` ignored `upgrade-insecure-requests` | Honoured from the policy header, or a `<meta>` in `<head>` for what follows it; not from Report-Only |
| 4 | `text-compression-missing` could gzip media a script fetched, or a part of a file | `image/`, `audio/`, `video/` and 206 responses are left out, as in Lighthouse 12; the media type is kept |

| 6 | `formaction` counted on any button, and scripts never fetched counted | Only a form's submit button; only scripts the browser fetches (not `nomodule`, not data blocks) |
| 7 | Some blocked loads were labelled as upgraded | Icons, tracks, image sets and images on an IP address are blocked |
| 8 | `hsts-missing` read the header loosely, and its message overstated the risk | RFC 6797's grammar, as browsers apply it; the message says a visitor *may* reach HTTP first, and the copy names the preload list |

| 11 | `srcset` was split on every comma, cutting URLs that hold one | HTML's parser, at most 50 candidates |
| 12 | Not probed: text in a legacy encoding | Probed (Chromium and WebKit re-encode, Firefox does not), and handled (above) |
| 13 | Smaller notes | The 14 days explained; a destroyed socket's null certificate; the sizes in a message add up; the 50 texts kept are those gzip shrinks most; image URLs redacted; Brotli is asked for over HTTPS only |

47 rules.

**Changes from the plan, and why**

- **Lighthouse runs in the render's own browser**, Playwright's headless shell, which Playwright names in its registry
  (`playwright-core/lib/coreBundle`, pinned with it). The full Chrome for Testing preconnected to www.google.com
  through the proxy and tried http: pages over https: first, failing them (the review).
- **Only the five metrics Lighthouse's score weighs** (`onlyAudits`): the score is the same, and fewer gatherers
  run; with the whole category, its image gatherer's 5 s timer held the process open after every run. A run counts
  as measured only with a score; a page still loading when Lighthouse stopped waiting is a timeout, without numbers.
- **Pop-ups are blocked** (`--block-new-web-contents`) and any other page is closed, so none escapes the request
  count, the worker guard or the bot's user agent, which is set browser-wide. Browsers start without any variable
  that looks like a secret. A launch that never answers stops within the budget, and puppeteer kills it.
- **Lighthouse and puppeteer-core are optional**: they add 111 packages, about 130 MB. Without them, or without
  Playwright, the scan goes on with a notice, and the CLI names what to install.
- **CrUX**: a 404 for the URL asks for the origin; an answer without its key, period or plausible values counts as
  none; 400, 401 and 403 say the key was refused. Each question gets 10 s; the fragment is never sent; a private
  page is never sent. `safeFetch` redirects no request with a body or an added header.
- **The copy says whose visits CrUX counts** (Chrome, users who share usage statistics and sync their history, not
  Chrome on iPhone; CrUX's methodology page), and the values read "or longer": LCP is floored to a tenth, and exact
  at the limit.
- **The browser SSRF suite's trap and hostile site moved to `fixtures`**, and Lighthouse runs through them too.

**Measured**

- A Lighthouse run of a local page: 4–5 s. ramsdgroup.com/ar: 4.8 s, 39 requests, performance 81, LCP 5.0 s,
  TBT 17 ms, CLS 0; the scan's score stays 96.
- cloudtopia.net and kvaii.com answered HTTP 402 to every visitor on 2026-09-28 (Vercel: "Deployment Paused",
  `DEPLOYMENT_DISABLED`), so they were not measured again.
- CrUX on real sites waits for the owner's key.

**Left as a design note:** CrUX falls back to the origin only when the URL has no record at all; a URL record
without INP does not ask the origin for INP, so `cwv-inp-poor` does not apply there.
